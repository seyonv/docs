Every puzzle in Base Fibonacci Division is a division problem in disguise. The game picks two factors and hides their product **N = divisor × quotient** across a two-dimensional grid as Fibonacci (Zeckendorf) tiles. Your job is to slide those tiles into the correct target arrangement — and when you do, you've proven the factorization.

## What Is the Game?

When a new puzzle starts, **N** is placed across the bottom row as a set of [Zeckendorf](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zeckendorf%27s_theorem) tiles — a unique representation of any positive integer as a sum of non-consecutive Fibonacci numbers. Scattered across the board you'll also see **glowing gold target slots** that outline exactly where tiles need to land.

Your goal is to slide and reshape the tiles until every gold slot holds exactly one tile and nothing is left over. Because each move rewrites a Fibonacci identity without changing the total value on the board, that final arrangement is a valid mathematical proof that **N = divisor × quotient**. No calculator required — the puzzle's solution _is_ the factorization.

## How It Fits Together

The grid's rows and columns are each labelled with Fibonacci numbers. A tile sitting at row-label **Fᵣ** and column-label **F꜀** is worth **Fᵣ · F꜀**. The bottom row (row-label 1) seeds **N** directly from its Zeckendorf digits, and the gold target slots are the outer product of the divisor's and quotient's Zeckendorf representations — together they sum to exactly **N**.

Every tile move applies one of the two core Fibonacci identities: the recurrence **Fₙ = Fₙ₋₁ + Fₙ₋₂** (split/merge) or the doubling identity **2·Fₙ = Fₙ₊₁ + Fₙ₋₂** (carry). Because each rewrite is an equality, the board's total value is invariant throughout the puzzle. Reaching the target arrangement is therefore not just a win condition — it's a constructive proof of the factorization.
